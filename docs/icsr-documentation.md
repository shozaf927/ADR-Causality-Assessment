# Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) Documentation

## 1. Purpose

An ICSR is a structured report describing an individual safety case involving a suspected adverse reaction, adverse event, or other relevant safety observation. Accurate chronology and traceable source information are essential for meaningful assessment and follow-up.

## 2. Four minimum elements

The commonly used minimum criteria for an ICSR are:
1. An identifiable patient.
2. An identifiable reporter.
3. At least one suspected or interacting medicinal product.
4. At least one adverse event/reaction or other relevant safety observation.

These are minimum case-validity elements, not a definition of a complete or high-quality case. Apply the current ICH guideline and the rules of the relevant reporting jurisdiction.

## 3. Patient profile

Collect relevant information such as:
- confidential patient identifier;
- age or date of birth, as permitted and necessary;
- sex;
- weight and height, when relevant;
- pregnancy status and gestational timing, when relevant;
- relevant medical and allergy history.

**Privacy:** Initials alone do not guarantee anonymity. Never commit names, full dates of birth, addresses, phone numbers, medical-record numbers, photographs, or other identifiable patient data to a public GitHub repository. Use synthetic examples or appropriately authorised de-identified information.

## 4. Reaction details

Document:
- verbatim reporter description where available;
- clinically clear reaction narrative;
- onset date/time and end or recovery date, if known;
- seriousness criteria and severity, recorded separately;
- relevant examination, diagnostic, and laboratory findings;
- treatment given for the reaction;
- current status and outcome.

Do not infer a recovery date. If the reaction is ongoing or the outcome is unknown, say so.

## 5. Medicinal-product history

For each suspected, interacting, or relevant concomitant product, record available details:

| Data element | Examples or notes |
|---|---|
| Product | Generic and brand name |
| Manufacturer | As stated on packaging or source record |
| Batch/lot | Especially relevant for biological products or quality concerns |
| Dose | Amount and units |
| Route | Oral, intravenous, topical, etc. |
| Frequency | Once daily, three times daily, etc. |
| Indication | Reason for use |
| Start/stop dates | Include changes and interruptions |
| Exposure duration | Calculate only when dates are reliable |
| Action taken | Continued, reduced, interrupted, withdrawn, unknown |

Record unknown or unavailable details honestly. Do not invent a manufacturer or batch number.

## 6. Clinical context and concomitant exposures

Include relevant:
- medical and surgical history;
- current illness and indication for treatment;
- laboratory and diagnostic findings, with dates and units;
- renal/hepatic function where relevant;
- prescription medicines;
- over-the-counter medicines;
- herbal products and dietary supplements;
- allergies and previous drug reactions;
- other plausible exposures.

## 7. Management, dechallenge and outcome

Describe the actual clinical action and its date: withdrawal, dose reduction, interruption, treatment of the reaction, hospitalisation, or continuation with monitoring. Record what happened afterward and when.

Possible outcome descriptions may include recovered/resolved, recovering/resolving, not recovered/not resolved, recovered with sequelae, fatal, or unknown, according to the applicable reporting standard. Do not infer an outcome from the absence of follow-up.

## 8. Follow-up and quality review

Before finalising a case, check:
- patient and reporter identifiers meet applicable reporting rules;
- suspected product and reaction are clearly stated;
- dates form a coherent chronology;
- units, doses, routes, and frequencies are unambiguous;
- serious-event details are documented;
- alternative causes and concomitant products are considered;
- missing information is explicitly marked;
- source documents and follow-up attempts are traceable;
- privacy and jurisdiction-specific requirements are met.

## 9. Reporting pathway

Submit real cases through the appropriate organisation, marketing-authorisation holder, national pharmacovigilance centre, or regulatory pathway. Follow applicable timelines and local procedures. This educational repository is not a reporting portal and must not be used to publish real patient case data.
