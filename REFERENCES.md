# References and Further Reading

The sources below are intended for learning and verification. Always consult the latest official version and applicable national requirements.

1. **Hill AB.** The Environment and Disease: Association or Causation? *Proceedings of the Royal Society of Medicine*. 1965;58:295–300. doi: [10.1177/003591576505800503](https://doi.org/10.1177/003591576505800503).  
   Original paper describing considerations for causal inference in epidemiology.

2. **Naranjo CA, Busto U, Sellers EM, et al.** A method for estimating the probability of adverse drug reactions. *Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics*. 1981;30(2):239–245. doi: [10.1038/clpt.1981.154](https://doi.org/10.1038/clpt.1981.154).  
   Original publication of the Naranjo ADR probability scale.

3. **Uppsala Monitoring Centre (WHO–UMC).** The use of the WHO–UMC system for standardised case causality assessment. [WHO–UMC causality assessment](https://who-umc.org/causality-assessment/).  
   Official resource for WHO–UMC categories and guidance.

4. **International Council for Harmonisation (ICH).** E2D(R1): Post-Approval Safety Data: Definitions and Standards for Management and Reporting of Individual Case Safety Reports. [ICH guidelines](https://www.ich.org/page/efficacy-guidelines).  
   Consult the official ICH page for the current guideline, implementation status, and regional adoption.

5. **World Health Organization.** The Importance of Pharmacovigilance: Safety Monitoring of Medicinal Products. [WHO IRIS record](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/42493).  
   Foundational overview of pharmacovigilance and safety monitoring.

6. **Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI), Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission.** [PvPI official portal](https://ipc.gov.in/PvPI/).  
   Indian national pharmacovigilance programme information and reporting resources.

## Referencing note

This repository summarises concepts for educational purposes. The cited sources should be consulted directly before using any method in professional practice. Reporting requirements and guideline implementation can vary by jurisdiction and change over time.
