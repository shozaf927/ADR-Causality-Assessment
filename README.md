## Project overview

This educational project presents a practical framework for evaluating suspected adverse drug reactions (ADRs) and documenting individual safety cases. It combines selected Bradford Hill considerations for causal reasoning with individual-case methods such as the WHO–UMC system and the Naranjo probability scale.

It also explains the core information needed to create a high-quality Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR): patient details, reaction chronology, medicinal-product history, relevant clinical context, management, and outcome.

**Scope:** educational learning and simulated case documentation. This repository does not contain real patient data, constitute a clinical investigation, or represent a regulatory submission.

## Learning objectives

- Explain why an adverse event occurring during treatment is not automatically caused by the medicine.
- Evaluate chronology, biological plausibility, dose-response evidence, consistency, dechallenge, and alternative explanations.
- Distinguish population-level causal considerations from individual-patient causality tools.
- Understand the WHO–UMC categories and the Naranjo algorithm.
- Organise the essential clinical and medicinal-product details for an ICSR.
- Protect confidentiality and identify missing information rather than inventing it.

## A practical assessment pathway

1. **Describe the event:** capture the clinical reaction and its onset.
2. **Establish exposure:** verify the suspected product, dose, route, dates, and indication.
3. **Check chronology:** determine whether the event followed exposure within a biologically plausible interval.
4. **Assess plausibility:** consider known pharmacology and recognised reactions.
5. **Investigate alternatives:** examine disease, infection, concomitant medicines, and other exposures.
6. **Review clinical course:** document treatment changes, dechallenge information, and outcome.
7. **Apply an appropriate method:** use WHO–UMC or Naranjo where suitable, explaining uncertainty.
8. **Complete and follow up the case:** identify missing details and follow applicable reporting procedures.

## Important scientific distinctions

- **Bradford Hill considerations** were proposed to help assess causal relationships, particularly in epidemiology. They are not a validated individual-patient ADR score.
- **Consistency** concerns repeated observation of an association across studies or settings. **Rechallenge** is a separate observation: recurrence after re-exposure. Rechallenge must not be deliberately attempted solely to prove causality when it could harm a patient.
- **WHO–UMC** and **Naranjo** are different approaches and use different category systems. Their labels and scores must not be interchanged.
- A causality category expresses an assessment of available evidence; it is not absolute proof.
